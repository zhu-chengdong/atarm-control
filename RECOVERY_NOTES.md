# Explicit camera-cycle recovery, 2026-10-27

Independent copy of atarm_controller_complete_cycle_20261027. The original and eight prior copies are byte-for-byte preserved. All tests are local WSL, PTYs and synthetic independent camera poses. No ROS middleware, physical UART, real camera or robot is started.

API: recover_camera_cycle(mechanically_confirmed=True, fault_cleared=True, replacement_transports=None), cancel_camera_recovery(), recovery_status(). Confirmations are explicit external operator assertions, never software proof that a mechanism was repaired. Recovery accepts FAULT or UNKNOWN, exclusively owns the cycle admission lock, and refuses all public motion while checking. The normal reference-confirmation shortcut is blocked when camera mode is faulted. Legacy explicitly motor-only tests retain their prior reference API.

Recovery only sends kp=kd=dq=tau=0 passive exchanges. It checks all four motors for new host receive evidence, stationary lengths/velocities, and a stable independent camera pose at the previously configured retracted goal. Camera reference, map, time, frame progression, pose and noise-margin checks remain mandatory. Failed attempts do not change references or erase the failed cycle. They preserve their own stop receipts; later success keeps attempt history. Recovery never automatically retracts, homes or retries.

Native quarantine is not cleared in place. A caller may explicitly supply two distinct, new, unused NativeSerialAdapter objects constructed from already-open PTYs. Only then is the host receive generation replaced and per-motor counters invalidated. New validated receive times must be after the recovery attempt. Native codec and C++ transport binaries/sources are unchanged. Persistent CRC failure after reconnection still rejects recovery.

The first passive reads rebaseline per-motor consistency histories to allow externally restored positions; a failed attempt still retains root FAULT and grants no motion permission. Only after all checks pass are the deployment reference, current hold targets, camera gates, endpoint cache, cancellation flag and both Jacobian estimators reset. Prior configured camera goals are retained; no new home pose is invented from a current picture. Success reports ready_for_retry=True and complete=False. A separate run_camera_cycle(plan) starts at VERIFY_START and must independently finish DEPLOY, BEND, NEUTRALIZE, RETRACT and VERIFY_END. In-process run IDs advance; restarting the Python object does not persist IDs, source high-water marks or fault history and starts at UNKNOWN.

A latent motor jam can look normal at rest. It may pass passive recovery even with an external clearance assertion, but retry motion must pass motor and independent camera progress checks and faults again if still jammed. This case is explicitly tested; recovery success is not a hardware-health certificate. Tension, gravity support, collision-free manual homing, physical stopping and real reference calibration remain unverified.

The ROS wrapper exposes recover_after_external_home_confirmation and cancel_camera_recovery Trigger callbacks and camera_recovery status. Calling the first explicitly asserts external home and fault-clear checks. Recovery service callback and state publication use stubs; recovery cancellation is exercised via the library API. Actual ROS executor scheduling and connection replacement require later integration. Trigger service success means verified recovery readiness, not completion of the arm task.

Evidence: outputs/故障恢复与重试验证_2026-10-27/full_run02. All 234 acceptance cases pass including 29 new recovery cases. A separate existing motor-only protocol limitation diagnostic is retained and excluded from acceptance. Recovery examples use no random camera noise, with a declared 0.1 mm bound and 0.5 mm tolerance; the margin test rejects an otherwise stable 0.45 mm measured residual. These are synthetic settings, not real camera specifications.
